02 · Access

Signing in as administrator

The account admin@toolvinementors.com is created and active. Vincent shares the mailbox credentials directly. Once you can read mail at that address, the platform sign-in is a single password reset.

  1. 1
    Open the mailbox

    The credentials go to the founder's Gmail address. Sign in to webmail, then change the password to one of your own under Password and Security.

    Open webmail
  2. 2
    Go to the Toolvine sign-in

    Open the sign-in page and choose "Forgot your password" below the form.

    Open Toolvine sign-in
  3. 3
    Enter admin@toolvinementors.com

    Submit the form. A reset link is sent to that address, and it lands in the inbox you just opened.

  4. 4
    Open the link, set your password

    Choose a new password and sign in. You land on the administrator dashboard with full access to every surface in section 01.

Why we do not email you a password. A plaintext password sent over email is a weak boundary. Sending you through the reset flow proves the platform's email pipeline works, gives you a credential nobody else has ever seen, and is the same path every member of your community will use.

05 · August changes

The nine changes you asked for in August

Agreed on 21 August, finished on the 31st. All nine are running.

  • Resources, public or members only. Chosen when you upload, so the public library shows only what you meant to share.
  • Clearer sidebar labels. Insights is now Analytics, and Resources is now Manage library, which is the page you were looking for when you could not upload.
  • A mentee can ask for a meeting. The mentor accepts or declines with a reason, and either side can withdraw while it is waiting.
  • Action items carry what happened. Marking one done means saying what you did, and the mentor reads it. Their own notes stay private.
  • Programmes run on rules, not typed dates. The site works out the next Family Meeting, Pray, Pillars or Equip itself, so it cannot show a date that has passed.
  • Rotating cards. The nearest gathering, this week's verse, and the current Vinethoughts. Nearest in time leads, and a stale verse steps aside.
  • Meetings for people who are not paired. Several attendees, a title of your choosing, notes and action items, in the same meetings list as everything else.
  • Testimonies. Members write, your team approves, first name only, and the author can take it down. Your team can also write one down for somebody who told them in person.
  • One tagline, and a way to stop the reminders. The same line on the site, the sign in pages and every email footer.
06 · Beyond scope

Built beyond the agreement

None of the following appears in the Statement of Work. Each was built because the platform is better with it, at no change to the agreed fee.

  • Invitations. A personal link that carries the role you intended.
  • Guided onboarding. A three-step intake covering contact details, focus, and where to find someone online.
  • Branded email verification. Confirmation email in your own livery rather than a generic system message.
  • Role approval. Someone asks to mentor, an administrator decides, and they are told either way.
  • Activity log. Every administrative action recorded with who did it, to whom, and when.
  • Visitor insights. Page views, popular routes, and devices, measured without handing your visitors to a third party.
  • Deactivation. Switch someone off and they are signed out everywhere, with their history intact.
  • Installable app. Members can add Toolvine to a phone home screen and open it like any other app.
  • Four extra public pages. Team, Outreach, Partners, and Get involved, none of which were in the agreed seven.
  • Community guidelines. A third legal page covering safeguarding, written because a mentoring platform needs one.
  • Partners management. Add an organisation, upload its logo, and it appears on the public page.
  • A resource in more than one chapter. A talk about money and faith sits under both, so either search finds it.
  • A shelf for each person. The library shows each member the chapters they said they were working on.
  • Vinethoughts, published openly. Every issue readable from the public site with no account, and it updates itself.
  • Notifications inside the platform. A bell on every screen, so nobody has to check email to know something moved.
  • Follow-up that runs itself. Somebody who stops halfway is reminded twice more, never at night, then left alone.
  • Verse of the week. A scripture band on the member dashboard that hides itself rather than going stale.
